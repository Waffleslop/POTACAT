**Headline:** A second POTACAT can now run your shack the way your phone does: pair a laptop to the station PC and its VFO window, HOLD TO TALK, FT8, JS8, SSTV, CW, voice macros and band scope all drive the radio at home. Also: rig presets that set TX power, RF gain and AF gain in one tap or by themselves when the mode changes, JTTY chats, a CQ that lands on a clear spot, and an IC-7300 fix for a spot click that left the radio in plain USB.

## Desktop to desktop

- **Operate from another POTACAT.** Pair a laptop with the station PC (Settings > ECHOCAT, same QR as the app) and the laptop becomes a full set of controls for the radio at home: the VFO window with every rig control lit, HOLD TO TALK with your choice of speaker and microphone, FT8/FT4 and the other JTCAT modes, JS8, SSTV send and receive, the CW keyer with text and the local paddle, your voice macros, and the band scope. The engines run on the shack; the laptop is the screen and keyboard. QSOs land in the laptop's log, and an activation started on the laptop is the shack's too.
- Pairing shows what expired and offers Re-pair, Remove really unpairs, and one log line says which connection legs failed and why. A laptop driving a shack refuses its own phones, so nothing is chained through it.
- **More than one of your devices at a time** (beta, off by default: Settings > ECHOCAT > Multi-client). Phone and laptop can both be on the shack; whoever keyed owns PTT, audio follows the last device that asked, and a Guest Pass is never shared.
- The SSTV window's template context had not been delivered since 1.11.0; the window now has its callsign, grid and park again.

## Rig presets

- **Settings for a mode, in one tap.** Rig menu > **Presets**: name a set of TX power, RF gain and AF gain (the radio's speaker level, so 0 silences the shack for FT8), fill it from the radio's current levels, and apply it from the desktop, the VFO window, ECHOCAT Web or the app. Presets are per radio.
- **Or let the mode do it.** Assign a preset to SSB, CW, Digital, FM or AM and the shack applies it the moment the radio moves into that mode, from any source: a spot click, the knob, the phone, or an FT8 session starting. It goes on before the first transmission and never while the radio is keyed. Hunting POTA on SSB at 100 W and switching to FT8 at 60 W with the speaker off is now the radio's job.
- AF gain is a new rig control for every radio that takes it. Thanks to the operator who asked for presets.

## JTTY

- **Chats.** The JTTY pane in the JTCAT window keeps a tab per station: double-click a line to open one, see the exchange as a conversation, type in the composer with the pair filled in, and log the QSO from the tab's menu. A station calling you opens a tab without switching you away. Message style FT8 only.
- **A CQ goes out on the clearest spot** near your offset, measured over the last 30 seconds of the passband, so a fresh session no longer calls on 1500 Hz where everyone else's does. Hold TX keeps you put. The move is made on the shack, so a CQ from the phone or a paired laptop gets it too.

## Radios

- **IC-7300 and family: a spot click no longer leaves the radio in plain USB.** When the DATA frame that follows a mode set is lost on a shared CAT port, POTACAT now reads DATA mode back and re-sends it, and says so in the log. Thanks IK4IDF.
- The WSPR map no longer covers the JTCAT window; the splitter sizes every mode's pane.
- A POTA export is named for the date of its first QSO, not the day you exported it.

## ECHOCAT

- **Linux shack on a tailnet:** pairing from away now works when POTACAT cannot run the tailscale command (snap installs). The QR carries the tailnet address read from the network interfaces, and the Settings card says what it found.
- The desktop tells a device which of your devices holds the audio, and the app's label reads "Operate from this device".
- An activation ended from a device ends on the desktop too, on desktops that understand it; older desktops are left as they were.
- The on-air callsign (G8LIY/P) rides the wire to the app, and the setup screen's Cloud sign-in takes email and password.

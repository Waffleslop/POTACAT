**Headline:** JTTY, the new keyboard and contest mode from WSJT-X 3.2, is in POTACAT as an alpha — try it on 14.090 and tell us what you hear.

## JTTY (alpha)

JTTY is WSJT-X 3.2's new mode: RTTY's feel with FT8-class weak-signal performance, 127 Hz wide, 1.9 s frames and no timed sequences — you type, you send, the other station answers when they like. POTACAT has its own JTTY encoder and receiver, checked bit for bit against WSJT-X's own tools: it decodes WSJT-X's test recording and makes the same call as WSJT-X's decoder on 40 of 40 weak-signal test files. What it has not had yet is a QSO on the air. That is why this is an alpha, and that is what we need from you.

- **Where:** More ▾ JTTY (or pick JTTY in the JTCAT window's mode list). The band buttons go to the WSJT team's preliminary JTTY dials — 14.090 on 20 m is the meeting place, 7.090 on 40 m. Note that 14.080 is FT4; you will hear 7.5 s bursts there, not JTTY.
- **Decodes are messages:** a line appears when the first frame decodes and grows until the station finishes. Click a line to take the callsign. Your own transmissions show in red.
- **Composer:** one line of up to 80 characters; Enter sends. A counter beside it tells you how many frames (and seconds) the message takes before you send, or why it cannot be sent — JTTY never trims your text.
- **Macros:** WSJT-X's eight templates come preloaded with names and hotkeys (CQ F1, Reply F2, TU CQ F3, My call F4, Their call F5, TU Now F6, Agn? F7, Exch F8). Right-click one to change its name, text or hotkey; + adds more, up to twelve. Shift+click or Shift+hotkey composes and sends in one go. Tokens: %M my call, %H their call, %E exchange, %Q next call, %G grid, %N serial.
- **Contest exchange:** Serial, Field Day (uses your FD exchange) or RTTY Roundup, with a serial counter that rewrites the exchange as it goes.
- **Tune, ATU, meters and waterfall** are all there, as in FT8.
- **Logging:** the Log button opens the Log QSO window with the station and mode filled in. ADIF has no JTTY mode yet, so contacts are written as MFSK with submode JTTY — the same arrangement ADIF used for FT4 and JS8 before they had their own. LoTW will not accept the submode until TQSL learns it; POTACAT says so once when it happens.
- **ECHOCAT Web** has the same JTTY pane in its FT8 tab, with the same macros. The ECHOCAT app will follow.

Known limits in this alpha: no on-air confirmation yet, no received-exchange capture into the log (type it), and the dials are preliminary — the WSJT team expects them to move.

## CW

- **Key-line hold outlasts a stale reply:** with POTACAT keying the radio itself (DTR key port, text or paddle), a receive reply the radio sent just before key-down could end POTACAT's transmit hold a few milliseconds into the over, and the power bar then read as the S-meter. POTACAT now trusts its own key line. Thanks LZ3AW.
- **Keyer timing in the log:** the ECHOCAT Web keyer reports its timing for each over to the desktop log, so a paddle problem can be read rather than guessed at.

## Radios

- **Test Connection gives the radio back:** on a serial, CI-V or Icom network rig, a passing Test Connection left the live link disconnected, so the next spot click said "Radio not connected". It reconnects when it finishes. Thanks KF0TDB.
- **"COM Port 10" is COM10:** a port named the long way is read correctly, and a port that is not there lists the ones that are.
- **FT-991 / FT-991A setup note:** CAT RTS must be DISABLE for POTACAT to hear the radio; the rig's setup instructions now say so. Thanks KF0TDB.

## Telemetry

- **On by default for new installs.** New installs share anonymous usage statistics — a random ID, version, operating system, session length, and QSO and re-spot counts per program; never your callsign, location, frequencies or contacts. The welcome screen shows the box ticked and nothing is sent until you have left that screen, so you can untick it first. Existing installs are not changed. Details at potacat.com/privacy-terms.html.

**Headline:** ECHOCAT's paddle now sends exactly the CW you hear in your sidetone, split and VFO A/B on Kenwood radios behave like the radio's own buttons, and the ECHOCAT app's Stop RX button stops desktop SSTV.

## CW from ECHOCAT Web

Thanks to LZ3AW.

- **The paddle sends what you hear:** ECHOCAT Web played your sidetone from its own keyer, while POTACAT at the station ran a second keyer from your paddle contacts sent over the internet. When a dot's release arrived late, the station sent an extra dot, so dots came out wrong at speed while your sidetone sounded right. POTACAT now replays exactly what your sidetone played, a fixed ~0.1 s later, so network delays no longer change your CW. If your connection has bigger delays, POTACAT adds a little more buffer and says so in the log.
- **Meters keep working while you use the paddle:** with a separate CW key port, POTACAT no longer pauses reading the radio on every key-down.

## Radios

- **Split on Kenwood radios** (LZ3AW): turning split on only worked from VFO A, and switching VFOs quietly cancelled split on the radio, so the display fought the radio for a second or two. Split and A/B now behave like the radio's own buttons: switching A/B in split keeps split.
- **SWR at 1:1:** a perfect match showed no reading at all, so the meter seemed to start at 1.3. It now shows 1.0.
- **Faster wattmeter:** the power meter is read about three times a second while you transmit, instead of once.

## SSTV and ECHOCAT

- **Stop RX from the app works:** pressing Stop RX in the ECHOCAT app now stops the desktop SSTV decoder and closes the SSTV window the app opened. If you opened that window yourself at the desk, it keeps receiving.
- **The app sees the real decoder state:** the desktop now tells ECHOCAT whether SSTV is receiving, so the app's Start RX button stays right when the window is closed at the desk or POTACAT restarts.

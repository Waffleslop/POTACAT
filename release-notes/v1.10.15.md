This one is named for **Bob, N7FQT**.

Bob has been sketching Activator Mode at me for a long time — actual mockups, drawn over real POTACAT screenshots, revised when he decided his own first version was wrong. When a feature request arrives as a picture of the thing working, with the buttons where they should be and a note explaining which quarter of the screen the panel belongs in, the hard part is already done. This release is his layout, built.

It started with **Dean, N7VBN**, who described a problem nobody could quite pin down: he gets to a park and hunts park-to-park before he starts calling CQ, and those contacts come out of POTACAT carrying the *other* operator's park and none of his own. **Charlie, W7RTA** worked the use case through with him over a week. Bob named the actual gap in one sentence:

> Where in the activator screen does it show what frequency to tune to receive another activator? That information is already sitting in the Hunter mode.

That's the whole thing. Here it is.

## 🏞️ Hunt park-to-park without leaving your activation

Press **Hunt** in Activator Mode and the live spot list opens underneath your log, in the bottom quarter of the screen, so you can still see the contacts you've made. Click any spot and POTACAT tunes your radio to that activator and fills in the entry row for you — their callsign, their name, their park reference, with the right program attached. Log them and the contact carries **both** parks: theirs and yours.

Three things sit alongside it:

- **Clear**, which empties the entry when the contact didn't happen. Bob asked for this as a convenience. It turned out to be a bug fix — see below.
- **A way back.** When you click a spot, POTACAT remembers the frequency you were calling on and offers a one-click return, labelled with that frequency. Take a break from your pileup, work a P2P, press the button, and you're home. It never moves your radio on its own: every QSY is a click you made.
- **ATU**, because changing bands to chase a park-to-park is exactly when you need the tuner, and reaching for it used to mean leaving the entry row mid-QSO.

The panel is a drag-to-resize split, and it remembers where you put it.

### The bug hiding underneath

Clicking a P2P spot filled in that operator's park reference — and if you *didn't* work them, that reference stayed filled in. Log the next station who answers your CQ and their contact went into your ADIF carrying a park you never worked. **A false park-to-park credit, uploaded to POTA.** Clear is the fix for it, so it's a button that needed to exist for a better reason than convenience.

## 🏔️ SOTA, WWFF, LLOTA and WWBOTA activators: your log said POTA

If you activate anything other than a park, every contact you have ever logged from Activator Mode was written with `MY_SIG=POTA`. Not missing — *wrong*, which is worse, because nothing looks broken until an award check fails.

It was a hard-coded value in eleven different places. POTACAT now records which program you're activating under when you enter the reference, and carries it through every path that writes a QSO: the entry row, the log dialog, the banner logger, WSJT-X, FT8, and the phone. Resuming a past activation reads the program back out of your own log rather than assuming.

**Two things to know if you've been working around this.** If you have been typing your summit into both the main reference field *and* the cross-reference slot to get correct credit, you don't need to any more — and you'll notice each contact now writes **one** record where it used to write two. That's the fix, not a loss. And a log containing both older POTA-tagged and newer SOTA-tagged records for the same reference on the same day will show up as **two separate past activations**; the data is all there, it's just filed under two names.

Also fixed: a summit or WWFF reference entered through the multi-reference badge logged as POTA against it, and starting a non-POTA activation from the phone lost the program entirely.

## 📻 FlexRadio: tuned the radio, streamed the audio, refused to key it

**N5ZC** reported his Aurora AU-520 receiving and decoding perfectly, tracking frequency perfectly, and not transmitting. Not once, not intermittently — never.

POTACAT was driving that radio over the SmartSDR API for everything: frequency, mode, and the entire FT8 transmission as network audio. But the one step that puts you on the air still decided "is this a Flex?" by looking at your CAT setting instead of at the connection it was actually using. It saw a CAT entry that wasn't connected, and gave up — after streaming a full transmission into a radio it never keyed. Every other symptom looked perfect, which is why this was invisible.

The same assumption bit two more operators in different ways:

- **KI4GT** runs a Hermes Lite 2 behind Thetis and Zeus. POTACAT decided any network CAT connection must be a FlexRadio, spent his session retrying a SmartSDR interface that doesn't exist, and then advised him to configure his Flex's IP address for a radio that is not a Flex.
- **K0BUF** ran AetherSDR on Linux with a Flex 6500 and reported that CAT didn't work in either direction. It worked fine — POTACAT had taken a **second slice** of its own, which is what it's designed to do when another SDR program is already open, and was faithfully tuning a slice he wasn't looking at. Nothing said so; the log line even claimed it was running with no SmartSDR open. It now says which slice it took, that your panadapter won't follow it, and which setting changes that.

## 🔊 Linux audio: the bridge that looked like it was hanging

**KF1G** reported the ECHOCAT audio bridge hanging on startup, through reboots. It wasn't hanging — it was failing, retrying, and failing again, and the error message that should have explained it ended mid-sentence with the word "Reason:" and nothing after it.

On Linux, POTACAT's audio device list had a section of raw ALSA hardware devices that **nothing in the app could actually open**. Pick one and every audio path fails: the bridge captures nothing, and FT8 quietly falls back to your default input. They're gone from the list. If you have one saved, POTACAT now tells you exactly which entry to choose instead, and every audio error now names its actual cause.

## 📡 FT8: move to another mode when this one goes quiet (Barry)

FT4 and FT2 can be deserted for long stretches. With **Switch FTx mode when this one goes quiet** turned on in the FT8 gear menu, POTACAT moves between FT8, FT4 and FT2 on the band you're already on when nobody workable has called for a few minutes.

It hops the **mode**, never the band — the three modes have their own frequencies inside the same band, so your antenna and tuner are never asked to follow. It only runs while Hunt or Auto CQ is on, so a radio you're just listening to stays where you put it. And because "already worked" counts the mode, the stations that just dried up on FT8 are fresh contacts one watering hole over.

## 🔧 Also in this release

- A **crash at every startup** for anyone signed in to POTACAT Cloud, which stopped device registration before it began.
- Pasting `192.168.1.50:4532` into a **Host** box — with the port right there in its own field — used to produce a dead connection and a DNS error every sixteen seconds. POTACAT now accepts the paste, uses it, and says what it did.
- The **ATU button** appears for operators with an external RF-sensing tuner (LDG, MFJ). It was hidden on radios with no internal tuner, which is precisely why you buy an outboard one.
- The Activator Mode duplicate check compared fields that were never filled in, so working the same station on two bands raised a false duplicate warning. Working park-to-park across bands is exactly what produces that.
- Pressing **Spot** while off chasing a park-to-park told every hunter on the POTA page to pile onto the *other* activator's frequency. It now always spots your own calling frequency.

## Known limitation

Clicking a spot in the **popped-out** spots window tunes the radio but doesn't arm the return-to-my-frequency button — only the inline Hunt panel does. Use the panel for park-to-park work.

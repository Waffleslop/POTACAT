**Headline:** Hunt gets filters: answer several targets at once, choose who goes first, never answer a station you name, and see in one line why Hunt is quiet. POTACAT now unkeys any transmission that runs too long, a Yaesu no longer keys up as POTACAT opens its port, and a spot click no longer changes your filter width. Yaesu's SCU-LAN10 network adapter arrives as an experimental radio type.

## FT8 / JTCAT

- **Hunt filters.** A Filters button sits next to Hunt in the FT8 window, and ECHOCAT Web has the same sheet. A dot on the button shows when a filter is not at its default.
- **Hunt says why it is quiet.** One line under the controls counts the CQs heard, how many were too weak, already worked, ignored, skipped or not calling you, and names the station Hunt answers next. During a contact it names the station Hunt is working.
- **Hunt: Custom answers several targets at once.** Tick more than one target in Filters, for example POTA, New countries and Watchlist, and Hunt answers a CQ that matches any of them.
- **Hunt: New grids** answers CQs from grids you haven't worked on this band and mode. The Wanted button can now show only new DXCC or only new grids.
- **Who goes first:** Strongest, as before, Farthest, or Needed first (a new country, then a new grid). Event stations still go first.
- **Never answer.** Right-click a decode, or type a call or a prefix like VE3* in Filters. A prefix matches every call that starts with it. Hunt, Auto CQ and answering stations that call you pass those stations by. A double-click still calls anyone.
- **A station that couldn't hear you is skipped for 30 minutes.** When a QSO gives up at the tries limit, Hunt doesn't call that station again on that band and mode for half an hour. This is on by default; turn it off in Filters.
- **Hunt no longer answers a CQ DX from your own continent**, nor a CQ NA or CQ EU aimed at another continent. There is no setting.
- **Don't answer below N dB has moved** from Options into Filters, with its per-band levels.
- **Hide decodes below N dB** changes only what you see. A station calling you is never hidden, and the Band Activity header says how many are hidden.
- **FT8, FT4 and FT2 spots tune the dial.** A spot names the signal, so an FT8 spot at 7075 kHz used to tune 7075 and leave the rest of the FT8 band outside the passband. A spot near a mode's dial frequency now tunes that dial, 7074, and the log says so.
- **FT4 replies can start late in the slot.** A reply tapped up to 2.5 s into an FT4 slot now goes out in that slot instead of two periods later, as FT8 already did. The end of the transmission stays on time.
- **2 m WSPR tunes 144.489.** The FT8 window and ECHOCAT Web had no 2 m WSPR dial, so 2 m WSPR tuned the FT8 frequency. A band with no dial for the chosen mode now says so.
- **ECHOCAT Web WSPR band picks tune the radio**, not only the decoder.
- **A band pick restarts a stopped engine.** After a voice or CW spot stopped FT8, picking a band moved the radio but never restarted decoding.
- **ECHOCAT Web's FT8 tab starts a stopped engine.** The Cloud FT8 view no longer stays stale after the shack goes idle. Thanks KC8ZP.
- **The Log QSO window a review opened closes** after the last waiting contact is saved or declined, unless you have started typing in it. A window you opened yourself stays open.
- **A DXpedition fox with a compound call is logged with its full call**, for example 6W/IK2HKT in Senegal, not IK2HKT in Italy.
- **The FT8 window's Flex waterfall scrolls on a quiet band.** A noise floor below the pan's scale is drawn as the bottom row instead of the frame being thrown away.
- **A reply from a chased or park station reads white on red** in light mode.

## Transmit time limit

- **POTACAT unkeys any transmission that runs too long.** Every transmission POTACAT starts now has a limit. When it is reached, the audio stops, the radio is unkeyed by every route POTACAT has, and a message on the main window, the VFO window and your phone says so. Before, a radio left keyed by a fault stayed keyed until POTACAT was closed. This is new and has no setting. The limits:
  - **FT8, FT4 and FT2:** 30 seconds.
  - **JS8:** 60 seconds.
  - **PSK31 and JTTY:** 3 minutes.
  - **SSTV:** 6 minutes.
  - **Winlink:** 2 minutes.
  - **Tune and the Station Setup test:** 2 minutes.
  - **Voice** (desktop, phone, voice macros, FreeDV): 10 minutes.
- **Not covered by this limit:** WSPR, the external tuner's carrier and CW text, which each keep their own timers.
- **A radio keyed without POTACAT is logged, not unkeyed,** because you may be on the hand mic. The log says so once per session. If a CAT-port line POTACAT holds is high, it warns on every over and names the DTR and RTS state, because that line may be what keys the radio.

## Guest Pass and ECHOCAT

- **A guest is told why a transmission was refused.** A guest whose transmission is refused now sees why, in plain words, in their FT8 window, VFO window and log. Before, FT8 simply didn't go out. Thanks KF0WWL.
- **The owner is told what to fix.** When the guest's transmissions are refused because the radio isn't reporting its power, the shack's log and a notice say what to check.
- **The pass's power limit is read back at once.** Right after setting the radio to the pass's power cap, POTACAT asks the radio to report it, so the first transmission doesn't wait for the next poll.
- **"TX power" in the FT8 log is the audio level.** During a Guest Pass the log line now says the radio's RF power stays at the pass's cap.
- **A phone showing FT8 gets the Flex digital view.** The phone's Pan tab centres on the FT8 decode window instead of running off the edge.
- **A phone's unchanged CW speed sends nothing to the radio** when it connects.

## Radios

- **New default: a Yaesu no longer keys up as POTACAT opens its port.** Windows raises DTR and RTS when a serial port opens, and a Yaesu whose PTT or keying menu uses one of those lines went into transmit on connect and on Test. Every Yaesu now drops both lines right after the port opens. With RTS/CTS flow control, RTS stays up and only DTR drops. This changes the default for Yaesu radios only. A radio whose CAT RTS menu is on then answers only with RTS up: after 6 s with no answer, POTACAT tries RTS/CTS flow control and keeps it if the radio answers. If raising RTS puts the radio into transmit (its PTT is on RTS), POTACAT drops it again, keeps RTS low, and says to move PTT off RTS or turn the CAT RTS setting off. Thanks G5HOW.
- **A spot click keeps your filter width.** A width picked on the rig panel used to be saved quietly as that mode's Filter Width setting, so one 500 Hz click in CW forced 500 Hz on every later CW spot. Through rigctld, every mode change also sent that mode's default width. Now:
  - A width picked on the rig panel lasts until the next tune and is not saved.
  - A mode change through rigctld keeps the radio's own width. A change into PKTUSB or PKTLSB still sends 3 kHz, because some radios bring up their data mode at 500 Hz, which loses most of the FT8 band.
  - Settings > Tuning > CW, SSB and Digital Filter Width, and rig presets, still set a width.
- **Widths saved by the old behaviour are kept**, because a deliberate one can't be told apart from an accidental one. Every tune that uses one names it in the log. To go back to the radio's own width, set that Filter Width to 0 in Settings > Tuning.
- **Full power (W).** A radio that POTACAT reads as the wrong size, such as a 5 W Hermes Lite clone controlled through deskHPSDR, which presents itself as a 100 W TS-2000, now gets a Full power (W) box under Radio Model in Settings > My Rigs. Enter what the radio makes at full drive. The TX Pwr slider, the meter and the log then show real watts, through Hamlib too. Leave it blank where power already reads right. Thanks W5LEE.
- **A Kenwood behind rigctld keeps its power at connect.** Hamlib's power reading on the TS-2000, TS-590S/SG and several others ends by setting the radio to its minimum, so these radios dropped to 5 W the moment POTACAT connected. POTACAT now reads and sets a Kenwood's power with the radio's own PC command.
- **A Kenwood that never answers is tried with RTS/CTS flow control**, as a Yaesu already was, and keeps the setting if the radio then answers.
- **The TS-890S is a 100 W radio**, not 200 W, on the power slider, rig presets and Guest Pass caps.
- **Icom radios that drop replies are sent one command at a time.** Some Icoms, among them the IC-7300, drop a reply when POTACAT's next command arrives while they are still answering. On those radios the mode and RF power were never reported, which hid PTT and HALT and refused every Guest Pass transmission. POTACAT now notices within about ten seconds and from then on waits for each reply before sending the next command. A radio that answers normally is unchanged. The Icom rig editor has Advanced > CI-V pacing: Auto (the default), Always one command at a time, or Never. Thanks NA4DQ.
- **The external tuner's tune never sets 100 W.** After the tune carrier, POTACAT put back the power it had before the tune; when it had never read the power, it used 100 W, and it kept fighting the power knob for a while. It now restores only a power the radio reported, stops as soon as you change the power, and gives up two minutes after the tune (GitHub #111).
- **SSB over DATA keys only once the radio reports the DATA mode**, so the start of each over is no longer clipped while the radio changes mode. On release, POTACAT unkeys first, then puts the voice mode back and checks that the radio took it. A radio that cannot report its DATA mode is not waited for: after the first over, it keys at once.
- **No frequency or mode read while transmitting.** A radio on a repeater memory reported its offset transmit frequency during each over, and POTACAT acted on it. A Kenwood (TS-480, TS-590, TS-2000, TS-890, TS-990), an Elecraft, or a Flex through its CAT ports still reports its transmit state during an over, so POTACAT sees a hand-mic over end and the frequency and mode follow again.
- **SWR and ALC go back to rest on receive** instead of holding the last transmit reading.
- **"The audio interface keys it" on FLRig and rigctld Network.** These radio types now have a Transmit (PTT) choice, as Serial CAT and Icom do. On a TS-2000 with a SignaLink, a CAT transmit sends the front microphone, so the interface has to key it. A setup note covers that radio. Thanks WX4MWA.
- **rigctld restarts itself when it stops.** The log names the exit and POTACAT starts it again, up to three times in ten minutes. Thanks LA8JKA.
- **Custom CAT buttons over Hamlib can send rigctld's own commands.** A command that starts with + or \ goes to rigctld as typed, for example +V VFOB. Anything else still goes to the radio.
- **An HF-only radio hands a 2 m click to the SDR again.** Radios known only by their bands, like the FTX-1, get their real reach, from 30 kHz up to their top band.
- **The VFO knob does nothing until the dial is known.** A VFO window opened before the radio reported a frequency could send the radio to 17.2 kHz.
- **The VFO window takes 432100 as 432.100 MHz.** A bare number typed for 2 m, 1.25 m, 70 cm or 23 cm is read as kHz, so 432100 and 1296100 tune 70 cm and 23 cm. Every other band reads it as before.
- **The first tune after connecting no longer cancels a split** you set on the radio (GitHub #113).
- **SSB over DATA sets the mode and frequency before keying.** On radios whose dial shifts with the mode, each over used to start about 700 Hz off (GitHub #113).
- **Filter widths follow the mode you tune to** on Kenwood radios and in VFO profiles. A TS-480 tuned from USB to CW now gets its CW width (GitHub #113).
- **The CW key-port warning** names your radio's own menu instead of the FT-891's (GitHub #113).
- **A reconnect waits for Windows to release the serial port.** A port POTACAT is still closing is no longer blamed on another program. The serial open line in the log names the baud rate and framing. Thanks KK6EZA.
- **A passing Test says when the port it tested is not saved yet**, so "Connected!" is not mistaken for done. Thanks KK6EZA, KO6BAH and NJ2DX.
- **A voice over at 0 W names the radio menu that is missing the audio.** When FT8 works but voice is silent, POTACAT names the menu: SSB MOD SOURCE on a Yaesu, DATA OFF MOD on an Icom. It looks only at overs where POTACAT sends the audio, never at the hand mic. Thanks KD9OUT, KD9WJY and KJ5CHK.
- **Station Setup checks voice transmit**, with a new Voice transmit step after the data transmit test.
- **Station Setup's test transmit moves to a frequency your licence allows.** If it can't go out where the radio is tuned, the test moves to a clear spot in the band's data segment, at least 3.5 kHz from the FT8, FT4, FT2, JS8 and WSPR frequencies, transmits there and tunes back. The log says where. If nothing on that band is in your privileges, it says so and doesn't key.
- **Setup notes for Yaesu USB drivers.** The FT-991A, FT-891, FT-710 and FTDX10 setup notes name the Silicon Labs CP210x driver they need on Windows. The Icom Network help names the IC-7300 MK II, and says why the original IC-7300 needs wfserver.

## Yaesu SCU-LAN10 (experimental)

- **A new radio type, "Yaesu LAN (SCU-LAN10), experimental",** controls an FTDX10, FT-710 or FTDX101D/MP over Yaesu's SCU-LAN10 network adapter. It gives CAT control, receive audio for FT8, SSTV, the waterfalls, the VFO window's speaker and ECHOCAT, and the radio's band scope. Test Connection names the radio, the FT-710 included.
- **There is no transmit audio over the LAN yet.** POTACAT refuses to key the radio while the LAN is the audio source, so it never sends a silent carrier.
- **It is untested on hardware.** It was built from the published protocol description, and nobody on the team has a unit. Use one program at a time (close PCC-LAN10), and use Tailscale or a VPN away from home. If you try it, please send a bug report from the Settings sidebar.
- **The FT-710 band scope** shows the SCU-LAN10 setting when that is what is off, instead of the driver card.

## SDR and band scope

- **The ECHOCAT app's waterfall colours on every desktop waterfall.** Pick a palette in Settings > Display, the FT8 window's Options or the Band Scope. Standard keeps each window's own colours.

## SSTV

- **Robot 36 uses the standard 150 ms line** when sending and receiving, so pictures from MMSSTV decode in colour instead of green.
- **Scottie, PD and Robot 72 keep exact line timing.** Each line came out 1 to 2 % long or short, so a picture from MMSSTV in Scottie 1 or 2, PD-90, PD-120, PD-180 or Robot 72 didn't decode. Robot 72 also had its colour porch in the wrong place.
- **SSTV starts its picture once the radio has really keyed**, so a radio that is slow to confirm DATA mode no longer loses the start of the transmission.

## WSPR and Winlink

- **Idle WSPR listens only on bands your radio is used on**, the ones ticked in Settings > My Rigs, and offers 2 m. If the saved band isn't one of them, it uses the first that is and the log says so.
- **VARA never keeps POTACAT's COM ports.** Setup clears any VARA FM or VARA HF setting that names POTACAT's CAT, PTT or key port, and POTACAT will not start a VARA that still names one. Sound card names come from the live Windows device list. Thanks N5XHI.

## Logging, spots and display

- **N3FJP confirmations go to the right service.** A ClubLog, LoTW, eQSL or QRZ confirmation from N3FJP is recorded as that service, not as a paper card. Confirmations that arrive later now reach contacts already brought in, and the whole N3FJP log is re-read once a day. The re-read never removes a confirmation, such as one from Update from LoTW or one you ticked in the Logbook, and never brings back a contact you deleted.
- **eQSL and ClubLog confirmations count as confirmed** in Worked All States, the DXCC tracker and confirmed-only exports.
- **GridTracker logs and spots each contact once.** POTACAT now sends the logged-QSO messages in WSJT-X's order, so GridTracker 2 no longer posts a second POTA spot.
- **A General contact no longer picks up the last park.** In ECHOCAT Web's Log sheet after a POTA spot, or in the Bottom Banner Logger after a POTA contact, a contact switched to General could still be logged with that park, counting as a false park-to-park.
- **The county goes to the county field only for US stations.** QRZ gives a county for every station; for a KH8 it is "American Samoa", which POTACAT logged as the county. A county you type yourself is always kept.
- **Portable calls resolve to where they operate.** W1AW/KL7 is Alaska, and KL7 and KH8 contacts take QRZ's DXCC entity instead of the mailing address.
- **The Activator log never loses or doubles a contact.** A slow QRZ lookup used to hold up the save, so a second press saved the contact twice. POTACAT now waits at most about two seconds for QRZ. A contact you type and log while the previous one is still saving is saved right after it, in order, and the entry row is cleared only for the contact that was saved.
- **A slow QRZ no longer leaves a contact without a name.** When QRZ answers after the contact is saved, its name, state and grid are added to the saved contact. Anything you typed is kept.
- **WWFF and SOTA references are no longer flagged "Not in parks DB".** POTACAT only has POTA's park lists, so it now checks POTA references only, and the warning stays inside its box.
- **The QRZ status line says what is wrong**: signed in without an XML subscription, a wrong password, or no such account. Thanks K0DHW.
- **A logger over UDP never gets a QSO dated 30/12/1899.** Thanks IK4IDF.
- **Settings are written safely, with a backup.** A POTACAT closed in the middle of a save, by an upgrade or a force quit, could leave a broken settings file that the next launch read as a fresh install. Settings are now written to a temporary file and swapped in, a backup is kept, and a damaged file is restored from the backup with a line in the log saying why. Thanks N8JMS.
- **One Change Theme dropdown** in the Settings sidebar, Settings > Display and the Welcome screen lists every look and replaces the light/dark switches.
- **The VFO window's radio monitor plays in both ears on a Mac.**
- **The Spots menu's submenus no longer draw over each other.**
- **A dialog's X can always be clicked** in a short or zoomed window.
- **Winlink and Mercury title bars fit the macOS window buttons.**
- **The voice macro panel stays inside the window.**
- **The VFO's "Type a frequency" box accepts typed digits.**
- **Up and Down step through Directory rows** in Nets, SWL, Landmarks and Channels.
- **The Log QSO dialog is wider** and its worked-band grid scrolls.
- **Saving Settings no longer empties the DX spots** from the table.
- **The same-frequency marker compares DX cluster spots by station**, so a different station on the frequency is marked apart.

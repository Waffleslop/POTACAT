The headline: two ways POTACAT could crash or misbehave on its own are gone — one of them triggered by nothing more than a signal arriving on your waterfall, the other quietly opening your shack microphone. Around them, twenty-one fixes from another heavy week of field reports, including rig-control bugs that have been biting every hamlib user and a macOS update path that has been handing Apple Silicon owners the wrong build for months.

**If you skip releases, don't skip this one.**

## 🛡️ A received signal could crash the FT8 decoder

POTACAT's FT8 decoder writes each decoded message into a fixed buffer sized for a normal callsign-callsign-report exchange. Two longer message types were added later — ARRL Field Day exchanges, and the DXpedition "fox" format (`K1ABC RR73; W9XYZ <KH1/KH7Z> -08`) — and nobody enlarged the buffer to match. Decoding either one wrote past the end of it and corrupted memory, which crashed the app.

Nothing you did caused it and nothing you could set would avoid it: it depended on someone else transmitting one of those message types within earshot. It could strike on any operating system — a newer Linux compiler simply arranged memory so the damage landed somewhere fatal instead of passing unnoticed.

Found from two kernel crash logs sent by **SP9LOP**, whose report contained exactly the right detail to identify it as a buffer overflow before a line of code was read. The buffer is now sized for the longest message the decoder can produce, with a test that recalculates that limit from the decoder itself so the next message type can't quietly outgrow it.

## 🎙️ ECHOCAT could open your computer's microphone

If POTACAT couldn't open the audio device you'd chosen for your radio, it fell back to "whatever the computer's default recording device is" — which on a shack PC is usually the built-in microphone. Windows reshuffles its internal device IDs when drivers or USB devices change, including across app updates, so a setup that had worked for months could suddenly point at nothing and grab the mic instead.

**N2FSM** heard the result from another room: his shack audio going to his phone, his phone's speaker feeding the room, and the room feeding the microphone. Turning the radio volume to zero didn't help, because the radio was never in the loop.

Beyond the feedback, a live microphone in your shack being streamed to a phone is not something an app should do quietly. Now, if the device you picked can't be opened, POTACAT sends **no audio at all** and tells you which setting to fix, stating plainly that the microphone was not used. Every audio start also logs the name of the device it's actually capturing from, so this can never be invisible again.

## 📻 Rig control

- **Every hamlib / rigctld rig**: noise blanker, preamp, attenuator and friends could turn themselves back on seconds after you switched them off. POTACAT asks the radio about a couple of dozen settings in one burst, and a bookkeeping limit meant the answers were being matched to the wrong questions — the noise blanker's "on" filed under Preamp, and so on, every few seconds. Reported by **K6RBJ**, who chased it across three releases.
- **Kenwood TS-480**: SSB filter selection did nothing at all, while CW worked perfectly. The radio has exactly three SSB filter widths (270 Hz, 500 Hz, 2.4 kHz) and POTACAT was asking for widths it doesn't have, which it silently ignored. It now picks the nearest width your radio actually owns and says so in the log. Thanks **LZ3AW**.
- **A real wattmeter**: the TX power reading was showing your power *setting* — which is why it never moved while transmitting. Radios that can report measured forward power now do.
- **New: raw CAT replies are logged** when you send a command by hand. Previously the app discarded any reply it didn't recognise, which made it impossible for an owner to help us add support for a radio nobody here has. That's the groundwork for Elecraft K3 transmit metering, with thanks to **jbkerkhoff** for volunteering his.

## 🍎 macOS updates go to the right build

The update manifest for Macs was being overwritten at build time in a race between the Intel and Apple Silicon jobs, so each release shipped a manifest describing only one of them. When Intel won, Apple Silicon Macs **silently installed the Intel build and ran it under Rosetta**. When Apple Silicon won, Intel auto-update broke outright. It has been flip-flopping like that for months.

Both architectures are now published together. The 1.10.12 manifest was repaired in place as well, so Apple Silicon owners get the native build immediately rather than waiting for this release. Found by **W3DFX**, from a report that started as "it updates, then it's still the old version".

Also fixed: a failed signature self-check at launch could park the whole session on a "download it yourself" link, with nothing logged to say why.

## 📱 ECHOCAT Web

A second parity list from **LZ3AW**, who is putting the browser client through more than anyone:

- **Custom CAT toggle buttons and sliders now work** — the web ignored the button type entirely and only ever sent the "on" command. Worse, editing custom buttons in the browser was silently wiping the toggle configuration you'd set up on the desktop. Both fixed.
- **The worked-station checkmark appears at last.** The web has always drawn it; your worked history simply never arrived, because above a size limit the desktop skipped sending it entirely. It now arrives in pieces.
- **The log form follows your radio** — it used to snapshot the frequency when you opened it, so a QSO logged after any retune went in on the wrong frequency. It also now shows **editable UTC date and time**, for QSOs written up later or off paper.
- **`{call}` in CW macros uses the callsign you typed**, like N1MM, instead of only the last spot you tuned. On the desktop this also fixes `{call}` coming out empty for anyone using the separate Log window.
- **Split TX frequency now disappears** when you turn split off, and **both VFOs and the solar indexes** appear inside the VFO panel instead of vanishing behind it.
- **The meter strip wraps instead of clipping.** On a phone, ALC ran off the right edge and PWR and TX audio were off-screen entirely — a mobile operator could never see the power meter at all.
- **Android display size is handled.** That slider doesn't zoom the page, it gives the browser a narrower page to work with, and several rows didn't survive it. Tab labels no longer overlap.
- A **Clear button** for the FT8 My Activity list, on both the desktop pop-out and the web.

## 🔐 Pairing that survives an update

Phones pin your desktop's certificate, and the desktop was regenerating that certificate — and its underlying identity — whenever a Docker bridge, VPN adapter or new DHCP lease turned up at restart. Every pinned phone then refused to connect, with re-pairing the only way out.

The certificate is now only reissued when something a phone actually dials has changed, and the underlying key is kept across reissues, so future rotations become invisible to paired devices. Also fixed: the desktop was advertising its **Tailscale address as its LAN address**, so phones at home dialled an unreachable address, failed, and quietly fell back to the cloud relay — the cause of **K6RBJ**'s "it always says Cloud connected, even on my own wifi".

## 🧰 Diagnostics and the rest

- **A crash now survives into your next bug report.** Because you restart the app before filing the report, the report used to contain the log of the *new* session — useless for finding what killed the old one. POTACAT now notices that the previous run ended unexpectedly and includes its log automatically.
- **JS8 on Flex Direct** finally receives audio. Radios feeding the decoder through SmartSDR were delivering silence unless the FT8 window happened to be open too.
- **Watchlist CQ tags** (`NA`, `EU`, `POTA`) only highlighted if you happened to have a chase target set — they now work as documented, fixing a feature that shipped broken in 1.10.12.
- **FT8 Hunt "Off" explains itself.** Setting Hunt to Off has always let the current QSO finish before stopping, but nothing said so, so operators reached for Halt TX and cut their exchange in half. It now tells you what it's about to do. Asked by **N2FSM**, new to FT8 and asking exactly the right question.

Thanks to SP9LOP, N2FSM, K6RBJ, LZ3AW, W3DFX, jbkerkhoff, and Ray — and to everyone who sends a log instead of just a shrug. Several fixes in this release exist only because a report arrived with the one detail that made them findable.
